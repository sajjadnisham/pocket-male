Reading the fili

Thaana is twenty-four letters and ten fili — the vowel marks that ride above and below them. A letter on its own is only a consonant; the fili tells you what to say. Pocket Malé teaches them as pairs, so the script stops being decoration and starts being readable.

Questions

Before you download.

Do I need to read Thaana first?

No. Every phrase carries a romanisation, so you can use the app from day one. But the script is shown first on purpose — most people who stay end up reading it within a month, and the fili colouring is there to speed that up.

How is this different from a translation app?

A translator gives you one sentence at a time and no idea what's coming back. Pocket Malé gives you the whole exchange — your line, the likely reply, and the reply to that — plus a note on how the situation actually works.

Is this Malé Dhivehi or the atoll dialects?

Malé. The atoll dialects — Addu, Fuvahmulah, Huvadhu — differ enough that mixing them in would confuse more than it helps. A guide comparing them is on the way.

What is Pocket Malé World?

A free browser version of the Local Market you can walk through and buy things in. Same phrases as the app, placed where you'd actually use them. It needs a connection; the app itself doesn't.

Is there an iPhone version?

The build is in review. The World runs in Safari on iOS today, so you can practise while you wait.

Do I need an account or a connection?

No account. The phrasebook, audio and review mode all work offline once it's loaded. Only the AI practice mode and the World need a connection.

Where are my favourites and review progress kept?

On your device. Nothing is uploaded, and there's no account to attach it to.
